**Decisions and disagreements with the spec (docs/02 wins where it is explicit; these are where it is silent or self-contradictory):**
- Bodoni Moda has no 300 weight (the variable face is 400–900), so the Statement style is 400 italic.
- Fonts are self-hosted subsets via `next/font/local`, as §2.2 asks ("serve self-hosted WOFF2"); the build-time Google Fonts fetch in `next/font/google` hit connection resets on this network. Arabic faces are the `arabic` subsets only and the Arabic stacks fall through to Bodoni / Manrope, so Western numerals and Latin names inside Arabic copy set in the Latin faces (§1.3, §2.9).
- Giant word on mobile: Bodoni capitals are ~0.62 em wide, so an 8-letter word at 22vw is 429 px on a 390 px screen. `GiantWord` scales Latin words longer than seven characters to the viewport minus gutters (CULLINAN → 69 px at 390). Multi-word texts (MAY BACH) wrap to two lines at the spec size.
- Scene word position on desktop: §3.9's rule ("baseline at 58% of the car's height") puts the whole word behind the car body; the sketch and the sentence beside it ("top of word above the roofline, bottom hidden behind the car") are followed instead. The word box sits 30% of its height below the measured roofline (from the cut-out's alpha bounds) and ends inside the viewport with a 2vw gutter rather than bleeding off with the car.
- Spec values are wrapped in an LTR isolate so "563 hp" keeps its order in Arabic layout (§12 of docs/03 for Latin names, applied to numerals too).
- next-intl ships no message bundle to the client; all copy flows through `content.ts` on the server.
- `MediaBg` gives `priority` to the mobile image and `fetchPriority="high"` to the desktop one; a media-scoped preload for the desktop hero is deferred to Phase 7 so mobile never downloads both.
- The eyebrow hairline is `currentColor` at 40% (silver) rather than the 10% white hairline, which is invisible at 24 px next to 12 px type.

## SplitText — headings arrive a letter at a time

**Built:** a per-letter entrance across every heading on the site, at the client's request, replacing the per-line mask of `docs/02 §2.6`. Two engines share one visual language (rise 26 px + fade, `--ease-out`, ~1.0–1.2 s a piece), chosen by where the heading sits:

- **The giant word — `GiantWord`, the same GSAP tween, slower and wider.** The word sandwiched between the background and the car cut-out runs `runLetters` from `src/lib/split.ts`, exactly as the section headings do, so the page has one letter cadence rather than two that nearly match. Its own numbers are slower, because it is the largest thing on the page and has to assemble rather than appear: **1.4 s a letter, 90 ms between letters, `power3.out`**, rising `0.35em` (a fixed 26 px under a 300 px capital would not read as movement). It leads the frame in rather than following it — the first letter is moving on the frame the word goes on stage, with no delay in front of it (the `delay` prop is still there, defaulted to 0). At the shared 26 ms every letter of a seven-letter word is in flight at once and the word reads as a block fading in; at 90 ms it assembles one character at a time — sampled per frame from inside the page, MAY BACH is `[0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0]` on the frame it goes on stage, `[0.37, 0.17, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0]` at +100 ms and `[0.72, 0.61, 0.47, 0.29, 0.07, 0, 0]` at +300 ms. `power3.out` matters as much as the stagger: the site's `expo.out` reaches 90% of its travel in a quarter of its duration, so letters look arrived almost as soon as they start and the cascade closes up. MAY BACH lands over ~1.9 s (visually settled by ~1.6 s), COLLECTION over ~2.2 s.
  The letters are **split on the server**, so the markup is in the HTML, CLS stays 0 and nothing is measured at runtime. Which engine plays them depends on where the word is, and the reason is measured, not stylistic: **a word already on screen is animated by the CSS keyframes, from the first paint.** Driving the Home hero and the four page heroes from GSAP put their entrance behind React hydration, and on a phone throttled to 4G and 4× CPU the first letter did not move until **FCP + 6360 ms** — the word sat blank for six seconds. On the keyframes it is **FCP + 259 ms** on the same throttle, and +74 / +134 ms unthrottled. The keyframes carry the identical numbers (no delay, 1.4 s a letter, 90 ms apart, `cubic-bezier(.165, .84, .44, 1)` for `power3.out`), so this is one animation in two engines, not two animations. A word that has to be **scrolled to** — the Home featured exhibit and the three Collection rows — runs the GSAP tween, because by then the script is long loaded and it can be triggered precisely; it keeps the 600 ms keyframe fallback in case it is not. `will-change` is cleared when the run finishes. Arabic animates a word at a time, for the reason below. The old `scene-word` clip-path rise is gone.
- **Section headings — `SplitText`, React Bits' component rebuilt on this site's tokens.** Same props (`text`, `tag`, `delay`, `duration`, `ease`, `splitType`, `from`, `to`, `threshold`, `rootMargin`, `onLetterAnimationComplete`), GSAP's real SplitText plugin (free since GSAP 3.13; 3.15 is already installed), but four deliberate departures from the reference implementation, all of them about cost:
  1. **No ScrollTrigger.** Every heading fires once, so a single `IntersectionObserver` does the same job without joining the scroll loop or forcing a layout on refresh. `threshold` and `rootMargin` become the native observer's, not ScrollTrigger's start-string arithmetic.
  2. **Nothing loads until it is needed.** GSAP and the plugin are fetched once per page during idle time (`src/lib/split.ts`, the shape of the existing `loadGsap`) and shared by every heading. A heading never scrolled to costs one observer entry and nothing else. **8.2 KB gz**, and first-load JS is unchanged at 103 kB.
  3. **The split is handed back.** On completion the spans revert to the original text node, so no page accumulates hundreds of elements or their compositing layers behind the reader. The one exception is the Promise headline, whose staircase indent is laid out on its line boxes — that split is kept, and re-measured on resize (`autoSplit`) so a rotated phone does not keep the breaks of the old viewport. It does not replay when it re-splits.
  4. **It cannot strand a headline.** The text is server-rendered as real text; if the plugin has not arrived 600 ms after the heading appears, the heading takes the site's plain 200 ms fade instead. Verified by aborting the chunk outright: 0 of 6 headings stranded on the home page.
- **Arabic is never split into letters.** Arabic joins its letters; a per-character split renders every one in its isolated form and spells the word wrong. Both engines detect the script and animate whole words instead (`ماي` / `باخ` as two units, letters intact). The same guard covers any heading over 90 characters, where a per-letter stagger stops reading as one gesture.
- **The giant word's fill moved onto its letters.** A letter carrying its own opacity and transform cannot be painted through an ancestor's `background-clip: text`, so the gradient is now a `--giant-fill` variable applied per character. Every character box is the same height, so on one line the result is identical to the undivided word; where the word breaks over lines (MAY BACH on mobile) each line takes its slice of the gradient (`--fill-h` / `--fill-y`, computed on the server) and the top-to-bottom fade still runs across the whole word.

**Removed as dead:** `RevealLines.tsx`, the `lines` prop of `Reveal`, `RevealWords`, and the `.reveal--lines` / `.words` CSS — all thirteen line-mask headings and the Statement now use `SplitText`. The Promise staircase moved onto the line boxes SplitText leaves in place.

## Speed pass, and the language toggle

**The two reported faults, and what they actually were.**

*Arabic would not switch back to English.* `localePrefix: "as-needed"` was paired with next-intl's default locale detection. Any document load of `/ar` — a direct visit, a refresh, a new tab — made the middleware write `NEXT_LOCALE=ar`, and from then on it resolved the unprefixed `/` to Arabic and redirected there. The toggle's English link pointed at `/`, so it bounced straight back to `/ar`; there was no way out of Arabic short of clearing cookies. `localeDetection: false` and `localeCookie: false` in `src/lib/i18n.ts`: the toggle is the only thing that picks a locale, `/` always means English, and no HTML response carries a `Set-Cookie` any more, which also makes the pages cleaner to cache at the edge. The cost is that an Arabic-preferring browser is no longer sent to `/ar` automatically — docs/03 §2 makes English the unprefixed default, and a toggle that works is worth more than a guess.

*Switching language on the home page jumped to the second section.* Not next-intl. On navigation Next walks the changed segment for an element to scroll to and skips any whose bounding rect is all zeros — which is every `display: contents` wrapper, and the Home hero is one. It therefore picked the Statement section instead. That only surfaces on a language switch, the one navigation where the changed segment is the whole `[locale]` layout rather than the page, and only under 768 px: at 1440 the Statement's top lands at exactly the viewport height and passes the handler's "already visible" test by a pixel, at 390 it sits at 1056 and got scrolled to 1019. Every push in `PageTransition` now passes `scroll: false` and the component lands the new page itself — at its `#target` if the link had one, otherwise the top.

**Three things had to be true for that fix to hold, and none of them were.**
- *The click handler has to win.* React attaches its delegated click listener to the document when it hydrates, before this effect runs, so a bubble-phase listener fired **after** next/link had already navigated with its own `scroll: true`. The curtain played but the push was a second, losing navigation. The listener is on the capture phase now; next/link sees `e.defaultPrevented` and stands down, so there is one navigation and it is ours.
- *The pathname has to be readable.* `usePathname` stripped a leading `/ar` only. The middleware rewrites `/` to `/en` internally and that rewritten path is what the hook returns during the server render, so **every English page shipped its language toggle pointing at `/ar/en` — an HTTP 404** — and no nav link matched its own href, so the active dot never appeared outside Arabic. Both prefixes are stripped now. This one was pre-existing and is the more likely thing the visitor actually hit.
- *The state has to survive the switch.* A language switch changes the `[locale]` segment, so React unmounts `PageTransition` with the old layout and mounts a fresh one — the instance that handles the click is never the instance that sees the route arrive, and refs do not cross that gap. The pending navigation is held at module scope instead.

**A crash found while verifying, and fixed.** Every navigation away from the home page took the whole app to its error boundary and rendered a blank page — both locales, both widths, nav link or sticky bar, and pre-existing. `PromiseStack` pins with `pin: true`, which makes ScrollTrigger wrap the pinned `<section>` in a `.pin-spacer`; the section is then no longer a child of the element React rendered it into, and React's `removeChild` throws `NotFoundError` on unmount. The teardown that unwraps it was a `useEffect` cleanup, and React runs passive cleanups *after* the mutation phase — too late. It runs from a layout effect now, during the deletion and before the node is removed. A cross-page hash link (`/visit#form` from the sticky bar) landed at the top of the page rather than on the form; that works now as a consequence of `land()`.

**Speed.** Lighthouse mobile on Home went **68 → 93** (LCP 4.07 s → 3.04 s, TBT 672 → 87 ms, Speed Index 3.94 → 2.55 s, CLS 0 throughout). Collection 95, car detail 94, `/ar` 89–92. The whole of that came from one thing:

- **The preloader was timed from hydration, not from the page.** Its 1.65 s ran from a `useEffect`, so a slow connection paid for the entire load *and then* 1.65 s more. Measured on throttled 4G with a 4× CPU: the curtain lifted at **6.46 s** and cleared at 6.50 s, which is what was wrecking Speed Index. §3.16 budgets the screen at ≤ 1.6 s and that has to mean 1.6 s from the navigation, so the timers are anchored to `performance.now()` now. Same probe after: lift **2.04 s**, clear 2.78 s, observed LCP 2.66 s → **1.31 s**. A fast load is unchanged — hydration lands well inside the budget — so this is only visible where it was broken.

**Bytes, all of them lossless.**
- `og.png` was a **1.14 MB PNG of a photograph**, over WhatsApp's ~600 kB preview ceiling: the card that every concierge link leads to was the one with no picture. It is `og.jpg` at **118 kB** now (`scripts/brand-assets.mjs`).
- The eight marque logos were served as raw 512×512 PNGs, **~50 kB**, into a tile that is 96 px on a phone. Through the optimiser like every other picture: **24 kB**.
- `imageSizes` dropped 384. It sat 1.5% below the 390 device size and the two together made the same photograph fetch twice on a car page — the hero at `100vw` resolved to 390 and the same file as a gallery tile at `86vw` resolved to 384. Desktop had the same pair at 1024/1440. That is ~20 kB on mobile and ~38 kB on desktop of the identical picture, gone.
- Everything under `/public` except `/fonts` was served `max-age=0`, so a repeat visit revalidated every file. Long-cache headers for `/logos`, `/brand` and `/og.jpg`.

**The masters were left alone, on purpose.** `public/images` is 272 MB of PNG and the obvious move is to re-encode it, but the delivered bytes would not change — next/image already serves 10–70 kB of AVIF per picture, and an audit found no image anywhere on the site delivered more than 1.6× its painted width. So the only gain is deploy size and transform latency, and it is not free: measured against what the site serves today, an AVIF q80 intermediate shifts the final image by meanΔ 1.5–1.9, and on the alpha cut-outs — the one asset where a fringe would show — by **maxΔ 255** at the silhouette edge. There is no lossless option either: re-optimising the PNGs makes them **17–39% larger**, so they are already tight. Re-encoding would spend real fidelity on a change no visitor can see, so it was not done. The numbers are here if deploy size ever matters more than the margin.

**Still open.** `/ar` is the slowest route (FCP 1.52 s against 0.91–1.07 s in English) because the Arabic faces are 67 kB preloaded against 40 kB — Amiri alone is 47 kB. Tightening that subset means touching Arabic shaping closure and was not worth the risk here. `sharp` is a devDependency; that is fine on Vercel, which optimises images itself, but a self-hosted `npm ci --omit=dev` deploy would fail on it.

**Checked:** `npm run build` clean, typecheck clean. All 26 page captures (7 routes × 2 widths × 2 locales) console-clean, no 4xx. CLS 0 on Home, Collection and Visit. No document overflow at 390 in either locale. The full navigation matrix (every route to every route) free of the unmount crash. The two diffs that did exceed noise were traced to work already in the tree, not to this pass: `src/lib/scene-config.ts` and the regenerated `mb-cgt-bg-m.png` / `mb-cul-bg-m.png`, all newer than the build they were compared against.

**Loading, which is where the care went** (`RevealObserver`, one observer for the page as before). A clip is a third of a megabyte against a photograph's twenty, and it costs main-thread decode as well as bandwidth.

## hotfix — the Worker stops re-rendering and the photographs become cacheable

**Built.** The live site was returning Cloudflare 1102 (Worker exceeded resource limits) while browsing:
images stalled first, then the next page load was an error page. Measured against the deployment with
`wrangler tail` and curl: every page was rendered by React **on every request** (260–393 ms CPU,
`x-nextjs-cache: MISS` on three sequential hits of the same URL), and every `/_next/image` response
carried **no `Cache-Control` at all**, so each photograph was re-fetched (2–2.6 MB PNG) and
re-transformed on every view — 1.2–6.2 s each, 8.6 s average under 40 concurrent requests.

Two causes, both configuration. `open-next.config.ts` was `defineCloudflareConfig()` with no arguments,
which leaves `incrementalCache` at `"dummy"`: the 37 prerendered pages sitting in `.open-next/cache`
were never read back by anything. It now declares `staticAssetsIncrementalCache` (read-only, which is
all a site with no ISR needs) with `enableCacheInterception`, so a page is answered from the ASSETS
binding before the Next server is entered. And `public/_headers` had no rule for `/images/*`, so the
masters were served `max-age=0, must-revalidate`; the adapter only marks a transformed image immutable
when its *source* says so, which is why the output had no cache header. `/images/*` and
`/_next/static/*` are now `immutable` for a year (both are content-addressed: a new photograph gets a
new name, a changed chunk gets a new hash).

**Measured after deploying.** Page CPU **262 ms → 35 ms** median (max 393 → 68), every route
`x-opennext-cache: HIT`, and `/_next/image` now answers `public, max-age=315360000, immutable`.
Nine routes checked in both locales, four captured in Chrome at 390 and 1440 — console clean.

**Left.** The transform itself is unchanged: a photograph nobody has requested yet still costs the
Worker 1.2–6.2 s, and on a `*.workers.dev` hostname Cloudflare does not cache Worker responses, so
that cost is paid per PoP rather than once. A visitor's browser now holds every photograph for a year,
which is what takes the repeat-view load off the Worker. The static conversion that follows removes the
transform entirely — the photographs become pre-encoded AVIF/WebP files served straight from the
asset store, with no Worker in the path of a page view at all.
